# Scene Brief

Use after the story spine and project `FRAME.md` are approved. This document
turns each story beat into an implementation packet without inventing another
visual system.

For every scene record:

- **story job:** what changes for the viewer and why this scene exists;
- **experience:** two or three sentences describing the visual world and
  intended feeling before describing pixels;
- **proof:** exact product capture, fact, quote, diagram, or media source;
- **start and end state:** what is already visible and what becomes true;
- **focal subject and depth:** foreground, midground, background only where
  those layers serve the concept;
- **choreography verbs:** specific physical verbs for load-bearing elements;
- **camera verb:** push, pull, drift, orbit, track, fold, reveal, capture,
  settle, snap, or another explicit action;
- **hold:** the interval in which the viewer can read the proof;
- **handoff:** the object, direction, sound, shape, or semantic answer inherited
  by the next scene;
- **sound cue:** narration phrase, music event, or restrained SFX cause;
- **FRAME citations:** exact project tokens and component names used.

Plan the film's rhythm before equalizing durations. Name the peak, quiet
passage, signature scene, and CTA landing. A scene is a world with a purpose,
not a card. Do not force atmosphere layers, decorative counts, or named
effects where the content does not need them.

The brief may name an effect class or semantic mechanism. Exact Registry
installation and seek-safe tween code remain implementation decisions.
