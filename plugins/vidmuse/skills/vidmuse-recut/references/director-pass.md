# Director Pass

Use this pass only after the full transcript and representative footage have
been inspected. Its job is to turn content into a film before style or effect
selection begins.

## Production mode

Production mode is a **density and image-ownership choice**, not a quality
ladder. Packaging films can be excellent; Director films can be overcooked.

Start in **Director mode**. This means planning the complete viewing experience
— including source-only passages — rather than filling the frame with graphics.

Use **Packaging mode** instead when:

- the user explicitly asks for restrained, overlay-led, or source-led
  treatment; or
- the approved brief already fixes Packaging as the production mode.

Keep **Director mode** when:

- the request does not explicitly select Packaging;
- the user wants official-sample, launch-film, promo, motion-film, or
  effect-first quality; **or**
- the argument benefits from planned acts, source-state changes, proof scenes,
  sound direction, or deliberate quiet.

If unsure, keep Director's scene-level reasoning and default individual beats
to source-only or light treatment. Escalate only named proofs; do not treat the
mode label as permission to over-design.

Record the choice and evidence in `video-context.json`. Do not infer permission
to generate unlicensed music, synthetic people, or external footage from
Director mode.

## Relation pass

Claims rarely stand alone. Where the argument carries sequence, causality,
temporal order, contrast, or dependency, stage the relation in time: what
appears first, what it triggers, what arrives together, what it leaves behind.
Present related items simultaneously only when the relation itself is
simultaneity.

## Act pass

Read the argument as changing viewer states rather than a list of sentences.
For each act, state:

- what the viewer believes or feels on entry;
- what changes during the act;
- which claim needs evidence rather than decoration;
- what the source footage already proves honestly;
- the energy on entry, at peak, and on exit;
- what the next act inherits.

Acts exist because the film changes job, not because a fixed number of seconds
has elapsed.

## Proof pass

For each major claim, write one visual-proof sentence. A proof tells the viewer
something the spoken line alone cannot show: an observed source detail, a
comparison, a causal structure, a transformation, a real product state, or the
finished output itself. Repeating the line as large type can be a focal action,
but it is not proof by itself.

**Density cap on locked-off mono with no B-roll:** do not prove every sentence.

- ≤1 full-frame Hero proof per ~60–90s (or one clear Hero on a ≤2 min clip);
- ≤1–2 light rails / indexes that reappear;
- majority source-led beats (captions ± whisper labels);
- cascade nets, dual timelines, pressure loops, module stacks, and night canvases
  are scarce — each needs a unique relation the plate cannot show;
- if system takeover would own more than ~⅓ of runtime, cut before style work.

Prefer the source image when it already carries the evidence. Mark that choice
explicitly as a source-only scene so quiet footage remains planned coverage.

When a comparison window must stay visible beyond the source edit that created
it, treat product identity as a state invariant. Do not let two proof windows
follow one later single-product shot. Give each window an honest independent
source segment or still, and verify that its identity remains correct for the
entire rendered hold. A loop-free final-frame hold is acceptable when the
frozen evidence is disclosed by the image itself and no motion claim depends
on it.

## State-ownership pass

Name the **semantic owner** of every developed visual state: the source image,
a proof aperture, a causal diagram, a title, an audit surface, or the final
identity. Ownership lasts for the complete rendered lifetime, not only the
entry frame. Before a successor takes ownership, clear stale titles, labels,
media windows, panels, and material surfaces unless their coexistence is an
explicitly designed composite with one unambiguous focal hierarchy.

Check ownership at entry, midpoint, exit, and the encoded handoff. A correct
timeline range is not proof that the outgoing state has visually cleared.

## Takeover pass

Decide the source state per beat: fullscreen, reframe, split, stack, pip,
composite, or hidden. Full-frame takeover is justified when it clarifies a
major proof, creates a chapter-level energy shift, or carries the signature
sequence. It is not justified merely because a visual mechanism is available.

## Mechanism-cost pass

Before taste selection, label every proposed substantial scene with the least
expensive mechanism that can prove the idea. Reserve GPU-heavy shaders, 3D,
DOM-to-texture scenes, and generative systems for one of four jobs: opening
promise, central proof, act-world hinge, or ending resolution. Write a simpler
fallback beside every high-cost proposal. If the fallback communicates the
same evidence more clearly, it is the primary design, not a consolation prize.

Do not name a Registry item in this pass. Name the narrative capability first,
such as `product-proof`, `code-proof`, `material-background`, or
`act-world-handoff`; resolve the implementation only after the film structure
is approved.

## Signature-continuity pass

When the content justifies a signature mechanism, prefer one object or spatial
relationship that can perform at least two different narrative jobs across the
film. Let its state changes carry the argument: a comparison axis can become a
balance line, a causal rail can become an expiry path, or a workflow rail can
become the final identity. Repeating one look without changing meaning is only
branding; placing unrelated hero mechanisms beside consecutive claims is an
effect inventory. Keep a simpler fallback that preserves the same proof.

## Output

Extend `packaging-analysis.md` with the production-mode decision, act map,
energy contour, proof candidates, source-state decisions, silent passages, and
risks. After coverage is confirmed—or autonomous approval is recorded—write
`STORYBOARD.md` according to `storyboard-contract.md`.
