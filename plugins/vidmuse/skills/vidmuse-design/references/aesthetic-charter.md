# Aesthetic Charter

What "good" means for a packaged film, stated once so every downstream rule
can point at its reason. Taste Gate items, hero-frame checks, motion checks,
and packaging tells all serve one of these dimensions; a rule that serves none
of them is bureaucracy and should be deleted.

Every dimension carries an operational question. Ask the question against the
actual artifact (analysis, showcase, rendered frame, reel) — never against
intentions.

## Maker's view — the film as a made thing

### 1. Intent

Every packaging decision can answer "why here, why this form." Beauty is the
visible residue of choices, not an inventory of effects.

> Ask: if this element were deleted, what would the film lose? No answer →
> delete it.

### 2. One world

The whole film reads as made by one sensibility in one world: the typography
knows the palette, the palette knows the motion temperament. This is why
`FRAME.md` exists and why it is the sole token law.

> Ask: take any two frames a minute apart and place them side by side — do
> they look like the same film?

### 3. Content specificity

Swap the topic and transcript, and this packaging should stop making sense.
The signature sequence grows out of this video's content, footage, or
metaphor — never out of the toolkit.

> Ask: would this design be equally plausible on a neighboring talking-head
> video? If yes, it is a template.

### 4. Restraint and rhythm

Silence is an active design asset. A dense passage earns a quiet one; an
unmodified plate is a choice, not a gap in coverage.

> Ask: where are the quietest twenty seconds of the film? If you cannot point
> at them, the film is over-packaged.

## Viewer's view — the film as an experienced thing

### 5. Clearer (cognition)

Packaging makes the argument easier to follow. Relations — cause and effect,
sequence, contrast, dependency — become visible, not just keywords enlarged.

> Ask: after one viewing, can the viewer restate the argument? Packaging
> succeeds when the answer improves.

### 6. More convincing (evidence)

Numbers, comparisons, and demonstrations arrive at the moment the argument
needs them, in the posture of evidence, not decoration. This is the reason
Director-mode proof scenes exist — and why they are scarce.

> Ask: does this intervention make the claim more believable, or only more
> decorated?

### 7. Felt (emotion)

Pace follows the speaker's energy; the energy curve has shape; the hero moment
earns a physical reaction. Flat films are packaged, not directed.

> Ask: chart the film's energy over time — does the curve rise, breathe, and
> land, or is it a straight line of equal beats?

### 8. Undisturbed (transparency)

The dimension video has and web design does not: the viewer remembers the
content, not the effects. Captions never fight the face; motion never outruns
speech; at any instant there is one focal point (a viewer tracks at most one
or two moving things). The highest compliment is "that was so clear," not
"those effects were wild."

> Ask: at the busiest moment of the film, what is the single thing a viewer is
> supposed to be looking at? If the frame cannot answer, it fails.

### 9. This room, this person (authenticity)

The packaging grows on the footage, not on top of it. Palette comes from the
wall, wardrobe, and practical light; graphics and camera live in one perceived
space; the person still looks like themselves.

> Ask: would someone who never heard the argument still say "this looks like
> that room / that person"?

## Distribution view — the film as a shared thing

### 10. Frame as poster

The hero moment survives as a still. Its developed frame could serve as the
cover, with one focal point, exact alignment, and no element that exists only
to decorate the template.

> Ask: screenshot the hero at its developed moment — would you ship it as the
> thumbnail?

### 11. Watchable on mute

With sound off, the caption system plus visual hierarchy still carry the
argument. Feeds autoplay silently; the film must not depend on audio to be
followed.

> Ask: scrub the film muted — do you still know what is being claimed?

### 12. A hook in the first seconds

The opening answers "why should I keep watching" before the viewer decides
not to.

> Ask: what does the first breath of the film promise, and is the promise
> specific to this content?

## Taste is the trade-off

These dimensions conflict by design. *Clearer* fights *undisturbed*;
authorship fights transparency; a hook fights restraint. Good packaging is not
twelve dimensions maximized — it is knowing which dimensions **this** film
sacrifices and saying so in the packaging analysis.

- A quiet founder's monologue rightly sacrifices 6 and 7 to protect 8 and 9.
- A launch film rightly spends 8's calm to buy 7 and 12.

This is why production mode is a density choice, not a prestige tier: the mode
names which side of these trades the film sits on.

The trade-offs have a narrow escape hatch, not a master key. When a craft
**anchor** (a duration, a ratio, a default) fights a dimension on a specific
film, the dimension wins — write the override and its reason into the
packaging analysis and self-audit. This never extends to hard caps and
counted gates (the proof-density cap, Taste Gate counts, Numerals & Claims,
the single-anchor rule): those are the floor the charter itself stands on,
and "dimension 7 needs it" is not a sentence that removes a cap. Changing a
cap is a user decision at a gate, not a self-granted exception.
