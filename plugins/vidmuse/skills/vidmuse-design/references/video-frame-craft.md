# Video Frame Craft

Use this reference when a project needs a visual system rather than a ready
frame pack. It governs what a frame looks like; the owning film workflow still
decides what happens and when.

## Start from evidence

Before choosing a look, name the audience, viewing context, emotional register,
subject matter, real footage or product surfaces, and the one idea the eye
must retain. Declare background, foreground, accent, type voices, material,
depth, and density before composing individual scenes.

Question familiar model defaults: cyan-on-black, purple gradients, gradient
text, identical cards, centered stacks, arbitrary glass, pure black/white, and
decorative stripes. Any of these may be correct, but only when the content
provides the reason.

## Frame, not webpage

- Establish one dominant focal point and one supporting path for the eye.
- Scale for the destination. Feed video needs larger type and fewer words than
  a full-screen film.
- Prefer zones, edge anchors, crop, overlap, rules, panels, and changing scale
  over a centered column of cards.
- Use real content. Numbers, labels, code, product UI, captions, and images
  must carry project meaning rather than placeholder texture.
- Let density vary with the beat. A quiet frame may have one load-bearing
  subject; an explanatory frame may need several coordinated layers.
- Decorative layers may create atmosphere, but removing them must not damage
  readability or meaning.

## Film-mode clauses

### `recut-packaging`

The face, gesture, or source action remains the visual anchor. Add only what
improves comprehension, identity, or emphasis. Do not force decorative counts,
ambient motion, device mockups, or a second focal point onto every shot.

### `recut-director`

The frame may depart further from the source when the approved treatment plan
calls for reframing, masks, image worlds, data, or full-screen interludes.
Preserve continuity back to the speaker and keep interventions legible at
normal playback speed.

### `create`

Build a connected visual world across scenes. Recurring motifs, surface
materials, camera logic, and transitions should make the film feel authored,
but every scene must still express a specific story beat.

## Color and material

Use one ground, one text system, and a rationed accent unless the project
evidence requires more. Tint neutrals toward the world rather than defaulting
to dead gray. Define whether surfaces are paper, glass, ink, plastic, light,
screen, or something else; shadows, borders, grain, and highlights must agree
with that material.

Contrast is a floor, not a style. Check load-bearing text without glows,
shadows, or background decoration.

## Composition gate

At representative frames, verify:

1. the squint test reveals the intended hierarchy;
2. the eye can travel without reading DOM order;
3. real content fits without shrinking below the destination floor;
4. the frame still works with decorative layers hidden;
5. repeated scenes share a world without sharing the same layout;
6. the result follows the project `FRAME.md`, not this reference as a second
   token source.
