# Film Spine and Act Worlds

Director mode uses controlled plurality: several visual worlds held together
by one film spine. Uniformity can flatten a film; arbitrary variety can turn it
into a reel. Both are failures.

## Film spine

Define these relationships once in FRAME v5:

- editorial stance;
- global typography logic;
- color relationship;
- material bridge;
- motion physics;
- sound motif;
- caption behavior;
- source-image policy.

The spine describes how decisions relate. It does not force one font, one
dominant color, one material, or one entrance across the full film.

## Act world

Each act world defines its narrative job, visual culture, material, typography
character, palette shift, composition behavior, camera language, transition
language, sound state, allowed mechanisms, and avoids.

Change worlds only when the content changes job. A capability montage may use
hard visual contrast; an explanation act may use one stable spatial system; a
conclusion may return to an earlier world with reduced energy.

## Coherence test

Ask three questions across act boundaries:

1. What visibly changes because the narrative changed?
2. Which film-spine relationship remains recognizable?
3. How does the transition make the change feel authored?

If there is no answer to the first question, the extra world is cosmetic. If
there is no answer to the second or third, the film is fragmented.

## Preset packs

In Packaging mode, one official pack may still define the whole project. In
Director mode, a pack may anchor the film spine or one act, but it does not
automatically skin every world. Copy its tokens only where the project records
that choice; keep official demo styling out of adapted mechanisms.
