# UGC video direction: demonstration, interaction, and mode-aware pacing

## Scope

For new concepts, first use [creative research and selection](./ugc-creative-research.md).
This guide develops product demonstrations and interaction-led concepts, not
a compulsory script order for every UGC. For wearing/style payoff read
[try-on](./ugc-try-on.md); for discovery/packaging payoff read
[unboxing](./ugc-unboxing.md). Those modes can use different openings, sound,
camera setups, reveal order, and closing actions.

## User correction

The first protein-powder test was a 12-second fixed waist-up shot with a
prefilled shaker and casual English dialogue. The user rejected it as dull,
without a compelling hook or short-video appeal. Do not retain it as the
successful UGC creative template. Natural wording and a realistic creator
appearance are separate from an engaging video concept.

The user demonstrated an alternative: begin while the creator trains chest or
back; handheld camera-operator POV follows muscle details, with an offscreen
question such as "多久可以练到你这么大". The creator finishes, gets up, turns
to retrieve the product, delivers an energetic product introduction, shows
powder and flavor details, prepares and drinks it, then gives a CTA. Duration
may be around 20 or 30 seconds and follows the content rather than a fixed rule.

## Develop the content before timing it

The following are creative jobs, not mandatory successive shots:

1. Hook: define what the viewer sees immediately and what question, tension,
   curiosity, useful information, or promised demonstration gives them a reason
   to continue. A strong visible opening can work without a spoken hook.
2. Response/reveal: connect the creator's reaction and product entrance to the
   hook. Avoid a detached attention grab followed by an unrelated sales pitch.
3. Product evidence: choose the actual details and actions that support the
   brief, such as packaging, powder texture, preparation, flavor, and use.
   Use genuine supplied product information. Do not fabricate performance
   benefits, personal results, or a slogan attributed to the brand.
4. Reaction: show the creator's response to what just happened. Vary energy
   intentionally; shouting, constant smiling, and forced hesitation are not
   universal requirements for human presence.
5. CTA: give the viewer a relevant next action. Match the real destination and
   offer; do not invent a discount, purchasing link, or promotional urgency.

Assign rough durations after those beats are viable. Allocate time for physical
actions and speech instead of arbitrary equal-sized blocks. Compress with
motivated cuts when appropriate; do not require every UGC to be one take.

## Camera operator and POV

Name the operator's physical position, movement, framing, and relationship to
the creator. An operator holding a phone can move in to follow an exercise,
ask a question offscreen, step back as the creator rises, and follow a turn
toward the product. The recorded picture comes from that operator's eyes;
it is not a third-person image of the operator filming or the athlete's POV.

Tie movement to information: an exercise detail shows the hook, reframing
reveals the creator's reaction, a product close-up reveals a detail, and a wider
view shows actual use. Keep handheld movement purposeful and readable rather
than adding arbitrary shake. A fixed insert may still suit a preparation detail.
Before adding movement, name the information it reveals and why the viewer
needs it at this moment. When that answer is weak, hold or cut instead.

Bind offscreen question and on-camera answer to different speakers and specify
their delivery. Allow the creator to react to the operator, then engage the
viewer as appropriate. This interaction can create human presence without
putting the operator on screen.

## Translate into model prompts

- Global controls: reference roles, one creator's identity and wardrobe,
  actual product appearance, location geography, rear-camera capture and sound.
- Ordered shots: viewpoint and framing, initial state, action, speech or
  sound, and final state or transition. Each shot has a clear visual purpose.
- Physical continuity: finish the exercise and return equipment before rising;
  establish where the tub and shaker are; show a coherent opening, preparation,
  closing, shaking, and drinking sequence when requested. Distinguish omitted
  steps across edits from objects magically appearing or changing state.
- Product introduction: adapt the user's requested energy or slogan-like line
  to the creator and actual brand brief. Keep it connected to the opening.
- Reference geography: the scene image supplies the room, not a compulsory
  fixed framing. Maintain positions and light across camera changes.
- Execution: map reference markers to the actual model's supported inputs,
  check duration and audio support, and split only when limits or complexity
  require it. Do not silently turn reference generation into first-frame animation.

## Review the result

Check the opening as a viewer: is there immediately something happening and a
reason to watch the next beat? Check that the selected mode's promise is
delivered: requested demonstration, meaningful reveal, reaction or result,
and appropriate CTA. Interaction is optional, not a universal requirement. Assess pacing and
speech together with picture, not only isolated frames. Also check identity,
product labels, object states, exercise motion, hand contact, speaker assignment,
and scene continuity. Report partial demonstrations as partial.
