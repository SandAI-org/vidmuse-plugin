# Product-led UGC casting and scenes

## Design logic

The user established that the creator should have a visible, credible connection
to the product. Develop a connected brief before generating references:

1. Identify what the product does and the action the viewer needs to see.
2. Give the creator a plausible use context or content niche.
3. Translate that role into physique where relevant, grooming, hairstyle,
   clothing fit, and accessories. Describe visible choices, not just a role name.
4. Place that action in a specific household or everyday setting in the target
   market. Set the rear-camera viewpoint so the action is readable.
5. Keep the character, environment, and real product as separate references.

These five starting examples use US TikTok as the exploratory market. The user
specified a male fitness creator for protein powder; other genders, ages, skin
tones, outfits, and locations below are creative examples, not category rules.
Adapt to the actual brand, audience, product format, and approved performer.

## Fitness protein powder

- Creator: a man around 30 who regularly trains. Broad shoulders and arms with
  moderate muscle definition, healthy proportions, styled short hair, a neatly
  trimmed beard, and a fitted charcoal sleeveless training top with black
  training trousers. Avoid turning ordinary fitness credibility into competition
  bodybuilding or adding a product to the white reference sheet.
- Setting: a used American home garage gym, rubber floor, bench and dumbbells,
  a partly visible power rack, towel, and a small mixing table. Side daylight
  from the garage opening and an ordinary overhead light.
- Camera/action: fixed rear camera at chest height, facing a standing waist-up
  position behind the table. Leave the lower foreground free for powder,
  shaker, scooping, and mixing. The training context explains the use.

## Eyeliner

- Creator: adapt the approved woman rather than replacing her identity. Keep
  styled dark waves and defined Western soft-glam grooming. Make a clean upper
  lash line and a fine wing easy to see; keep surrounding shadow restrained.
  Hair clears the eyes. A fitted black camisole, dark straight jeans, and small
  gold accessories create a prepared beauty-creator look.
- Setting: a city apartment's everyday vanity with a small makeup bag, brushes,
  and normal bedroom traces. Soft side-window light reaches the face position.
- Camera/action: fixed close rear-camera view at seated face height, with only
  a small strip of desk below for the pen and hands. Use the portrait crop in
  the character reference for makeup detail. Decide in the video brief whether
  the shot demonstrates application, one-eye comparison, or the finished look;
  a finished-look sheet does not itself define a before state.

## Kitchen cleaner

- Creator: a home/lifestyle creator around 35, with groomed low ponytail,
  defined everyday makeup, a fitted sage-green T-shirt, cream straight jeans,
  short tidy nails, and simple jewelry. She is sharing her household routine;
  a cleaner product does not imply a professional-cleaner costume.
- Setting: a used family kitchen, frame-style cabinets, tiled backsplash, a
  glass cooktop and counter with limited cooking grease. Keep a pan, towel,
  oil bottle, and paper roll away from the demonstration area.
- Camera/action: rear camera above the work surface, tilted down enough to
  show spraying, wiping, and the result on the same visible surface. Side light
  reveals the residue. Include the actual product instructions when developing
  the demonstration; surface compatibility and product behavior come from the
  supplied product, not an invented visual effect.

## Fried chicken

- Creator: a personable food reviewer around 29, styled short hair, tidy
  stubble, a structured relaxed burgundy T-shirt, black cargo trousers, and a
  simple chain. Give him expressive eyes and a healthy ordinary build rather
  than a stereotype based on the food category.
- Setting: an evening takeout meal in a city apartment, a dark table, paper
  packaging, napkins, a cup, a lived-in sofa, and a practical warm lamp.
- Camera/action: fixed rear camera at seated eye height, waist-up distance;
  the lower third shows table space for the actual box, chicken, and hands.
  Leave the central eating area clear. The food and packaging appearance come
  from the product reference, not a generic food invented in the location image.

## Headphones

- Creator: a music/technology lifestyle creator around 27, styled curls gathered
  up so the ears are visible, prepared makeup, a fitted white short T-shirt,
  charcoal casual trousers, and small silver accessories. Adapt hair and
  accessories to the actual in-ear or over-ear product; don't put a generic
  headphone on the character sheet.
- Setting: an apartment music/work desk with a laptop, small speaker, audio
  interface, coherent cables, records, notebook, and a jacket over the chair.
  A few used items establish routine without turning the room into a showroom.
- Camera/action: fixed rear camera at seated eye height, waist-up distance.
  Clear desk foreground for the real packaging, product close-ups, and hands;
  the performer has room to put on the headphones and react while listening.

## Review

Check the actual outputs for white-sheet layout, identity and outfit continuity,
adult proportions, and category-relevant styling. Check each scene separately
for camera geometry, readable action surfaces, believable light, and room for
the performer. A category label in the prompt is not evidence that the generated
image supports its planned demonstration. Record any limitation before using
the references to generate video.
