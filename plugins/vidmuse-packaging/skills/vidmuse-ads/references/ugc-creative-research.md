# UGC creative research and selection

## When this applies

Use for a new concept or a request to improve creative strength, hook, local
voice, or platform fit. For a narrow change to approved material, preserve
the concept and research only the dimension that needs updating. Do not add
paid generations, publishing, or mandatory user approvals to the research step.

## Establish the brief and mode

Resolve the actual market and audience, advertising objective, supplied
product facts, creator/use context, and main viewer payoff. Choose try-on,
unboxing, demonstration, tasting, review, talking, or a purposeful combination.
Read the corresponding mode guidance before interpreting examples. Product
category alone does not choose the format.

Separate what can be visibly demonstrated from unsupported functions or claims.
An attractive product image may support a style/try-on concept without proving
durability, comfort, UV protection, or performance. Choose a concept the available
evidence can support; let it determine necessary casting, wardrobe, and location.

## Gather useful evidence

Use a small relevant sample, expanding only when it lacks useful diversity:

- Same-category organic creator videos and real comments: audience situations,
  language, questions, preferences, and reactions.
- Competitor ads in the target market: attention mechanisms, demonstrations,
  buying objections, CTA, and repeated category conventions.
- Adjacent-category content: a useful narrative or interaction mechanism that
  can transfer without copying another product's plot or claims.
- Current platform trends: language, community context, topic, or format when
  relevant. Record market and date. Trend participation is optional, not a
  substitute for a product-specific idea.

TikTok Creative Center provides starting points:

- [Top Ads documentation](https://ads-useast2a.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/how-to-use-the-top-ads-dashboard):
  region/industry/objective filters, available viewing/conversion metrics, and
  relative second-by-second performance. Check current access and filters.
- [Trends documentation](https://ads.us.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/how-to-use-trends?lang=en&redirected=2):
  topic trends, related videos, audience and regional context.

These resources were checked on 2026-10-09; availability may change. Top Ads
is a selected collection, not a complete competitor inventory. Organic views
or comments are not conversion evidence; reported ad metrics do not establish
that one hook caused the result. Do not infer missing metrics.

Inspect the actual video, especially its opening and payoff, rather than relying
on a thumbnail, hashtag, search snippet, or a generic "best hooks" article.
Listen or inspect available captions/transcripts to examine language. Record
what was actually accessible: full video/audio, frames, captions, or text only.
If access is limited, retain that limitation and use supported observations;
do not claim to have watched/heard it or to have verified a current trend.

## Extract a mechanism, not a line to copy

For each useful example, record its source/date/market/mode and briefly answer:

1. What is visible and audible immediately?
2. What interest, question, expectation, or decision does it create?
3. Why is that relevant to this audience and product?
4. How does the next beat advance it, and where is the payoff?
5. What do camera, edits, sound, and reaction contribute?
6. What can transfer, and what belongs only to this creator, product, or trend?

Keep original creative proposals separate from observations. Do not present an
invented line as a popular local expression. Do not copy distinctive dialogue,
branding, or unsupported experiences from references.

## Compare directions before choosing

Normally sketch three genuinely different mechanisms in a few lines each;
scale this to the task. Different adjectives or hook sentences attached to
the same plot do not constitute distinct concepts. For each, state the opening
picture and sound, viewer reason to continue, product connection, payoff,
mode-appropriate CTA, and why it is feasible with the actual inputs.

Select using explicit reasons, not an invented numerical prediction of virality.
Do not ask the user to approve routine internal choices unless they requested
selection or a missing material fact requires input. Then develop the winning
concept into shots and allocate time from content.

## Reject weak concepts before spending

- Empty hook: "Wait", "Watch this", or "You need this" supplies no concrete
  reason to care. Such phrases can introduce a strong visual, but cannot carry
  the concept alone.
- Weak payoff: the outcome is obvious and offers neither meaningful comparison,
  useful detail, personality, nor an interesting decision.
- Detached hook: it grabs attention but the reveal does not answer its promise.
- Generic product connection: changing the product would leave every important
  beat unchanged. A shared format is fine; the decisive evidence must belong
  to the actual product.
- Camera without a job: specify what new information becomes visible and why
  now. "Step forward", "orbit", or "add handheld" alone does not justify motion.
- Unsupported evidence: the concept depends on facts, packaging, results,
  claims, or a comparison absent from the brief.
- Format mismatch: a try-on hides the worn effect, or an unboxing hides the
  discovery and jumps straight to a generic sales presentation.

Rewrite the weak dimension rather than adding more technical shot controls.
For each shot, finish: "This view lets the viewer notice ___, which advances
___". A hold or simple cut may be better than motion. Review the generated
opening and payoff again; a detailed prompt does not prove creative success.
